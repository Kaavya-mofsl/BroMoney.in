Framework Overview

What is BOX Theory?

Six dimensions. One score out of 60. A structured way to judge any listed company's long-term investment quality.

0 Max Score

The 6 Dimensions

Each dimension scores 0–10. Every dimension counts equally — no weighting.

open_with

Dimension 1

Size

How large is the business today — revenue, profit, market cap, and reach?

↑ Market leader with proven scale and pricing power.
↓ Early-stage or niche — higher risk, more room to grow.

Key Metrics

Market Cap Revenue Net Profit Asset Base Geo Reach
lightbulb

Dimension 2

Usefulness

Is the product or service genuinely essential, or built on hype?

↑ Solves a real, recurring need (banking, pharma, telecom).
↓ Fad-driven or easily replaceable demand.

Key Questions

Recurring Demand Essential Sector 10-Year Relevance
shield

Dimension 3

Strength

Can the company survive recessions and rate cycles without lasting damage?

↑ Low debt, strong cash flows, healthy ROCE/ROE and margins.
↓ Heavy leverage, thin margins, weak or volatile cash generation.

Key Metrics

ROCE ROE Debt/Equity Int. Coverage EBITDA Margin Free Cash Flow
transform

Dimension 4

Versatility

Does the business have multiple revenue streams, or is it dangerously concentrated?

↑ Multiple products, geographies, and adjacent market opportunities.
↓ Single product, customer, or geography — fragile by design.

Key Areas

Product Mix Customer Base Geo Diversification Innovation
layers

Dimension 5

Composition

Is the management trustworthy? Are the books clean and profits real?

↑ Trustworthy promoters, clean audit, genuine profits, low pledging.
↓ High pledge, related-party deals, auditor flags, PAT/CFO mismatch.

Key Indicators

Promoter Holding Promoter Pledge Auditor Quality Cash vs PAT
rocket_launch

Dimension 6

Capacity

How much larger can this business realistically become in the next 3–5 years?

↑ Large TAM, active expansion, operating leverage ahead.
↓ Saturated market, no capex, fully utilised with nowhere to grow.

Key Areas

Industry TAM Cap. Utilisation Expansion Plans Revenue CAGR

Common Mistakes

Four ways investors misread BOX Theory scores.

Mistake 01

Treating a high score as a buy signal

BOX Theory measures quality, not valuation. A Strong Box at 3× book may still be a poor entry — always layer in price analysis.

Mistake 02

Ignoring a single very low dimension

A 2/10 in Composition is a red flag regardless of the total. One broken dimension can unwind all others.

Mistake 03

Running the analysis only once

Scores change as businesses evolve. Re-run after every quarterly result — a 35 today can become a 48 or a 22.

Mistake 04

Equating Size with quality

A large company can still be a Poor Box. A ₹5,000 Cr company with great Strength and Composition can outperform a ₹5L Cr one.
